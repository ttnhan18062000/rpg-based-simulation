Don't use SimQ to verify a narrow mechanic or an edge case. It measures **broad emergent simulation
quality** across a corpus — pillars, grades, drift. That is a different question from "does this
specific mechanic work."

Stated by the user 2026-09-15, alongside the direction to build a production-like mechanic-
verification component.

**What SimQ IS for**, in the user's own words: **balancing, and observing major parts of the
simulation.** Broad behaviour over a whole run — is the economy working, is the world interesting,
did the numbers drift. That is its range; stay inside it.

**The division:**
- **SimQ** — balance and major-part observation. Corpus-wide, emergent, pillar-scored.
- **Focused scenarios** (`src/certification/scenarios.py` + `CertificationHarness`, and the general
  component being built from it) — does *this mechanic* work when its conditions are met? Small,
  production-like (real catalog, real compile path, real pipeline), deterministic.

**Why it matters, measured:** the SimQ calibration corpus runs at ~10 entities, so a change that
halved combat at 1000 entities produced **no pillar movement at all**
(`TCK-20260915-SIMQ-CORPUS-BLIND-TO-SCALE-DEPENDENT-BEHAVIOR`). Citing SimQ green as evidence a
narrow change is safe is citing an instrument outside its range.

Related: [[feedback-simq-no-forced-routes]] — don't force an unrealistic route to fix a pillar
score; investigate whether it's a bug or correct behaviour first.
