When a peer reports a striking rate from a probe still in progress, ask **what population it
counted** before repeating it. On 2026-10-05 `rpg-implementer` reported "the target was already dead
in 3078 of 6891 samples (45%)" while an objective was still ACTIVE. I relayed it to the user as an
established finding. It retracted the figure within the hour: the probe had not excluded dead
*holders*, and dead entities keep their strategic state, so it had counted dead heroes whose
objective was irrelevant. A live-holder filter gave **28** samples, not 3078 — a different finding,
not a refinement.

**Why:** the number arrived as part of a competent, well-argued report with a correct conclusion
attached, which is exactly when the denominator goes unexamined. A striking rate on a fresh probe is
more often a population-definition artifact than a real effect.

**How to apply:** for any peer rate, ask "which entities/rows were included, and which excluded?"
before it enters a ticket, a PR body, or a report to the user. Attribute it as in-progress
("it measured X, not yet verified") rather than as fact. And when the retraction comes, correct it
in every place it was relayed — say plainly that I propagated it, rather than attributing the whole
error to the peer. Strengthens [[feedback_verify_a_peer_finding_before_relaying_it_as_fact]];
see also [[feedback_a_probe_is_an_instrument_that_can_be_wrong]] and
[[feedback_absent_from_sample_is_not_unreachable]].
