A green test proves the code satisfies **the fixture**, not the world. When a test asserts a *spatial or
dimensional* property, check the fixture's numbers against the real corpus before trusting it.

**Confirmed 2026-10-03, the raid fix.** I wrote
`test_global_raid_raiders_land_inside_a_region` to discharge an AC that raiders must spawn inside a
region, and it passed. A corpus measurement then showed **11 of 12 real spawns were still outside every
region.** The reason was arithmetic in the fixture: my region was `(100,100,160,160)` — 60 wide,
half-width 30 — while the spawn ring is `SANCTUARY_RADIUS + 10 = 25`, so a ring around a centred city
fits inside it. Every real corpus region is ~30 wide (`hometown` is `[10,10,40,40]`, half-width 15), so
a radius-25 ring **necessarily** lands outside. My test asserted a property no shipped world satisfies.

I had picked 60 to make the region "clearly not at the origin", with no thought about the ring radius.

**Why:** this failure is invisible from inside the test — it passes, the positive control (fail on the
unfixed code) also passes, and the AC looks discharged. Only a corpus-level measurement catches it, and
by then the fix is built. It is the dimensional cousin of
[[feedback_verify_with_the_command_ci_runs_not_the_authors_filter]]: both are "I verified the wrong
thing and the number looked right".

**How to apply:** when a test turns on a distance, radius, bound, or capacity, write down the fixture's
value **and the corpus's value** and compare them before claiming the AC. Prefer fixture dimensions
copied from a real world (`WorldRepository.load_world_with_context` + `WorldCompiler.compile`, then read
the bounds) over invented round numbers. If the property only holds for generous dimensions, that is
itself the finding — here it meant the fix could not work at all, which pointed at retirement rather
than at a bigger fixture. See [[feedback_frozen_fixture_minimal_subset]] for the related sizing rule.
