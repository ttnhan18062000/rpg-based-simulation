Review Brief: City/Region Split, Group & Country Lifecycle Audits, Cross-Wiring
================================================================================

This is a review, not an open discussion (a discussion brief on the layer
model already happened and produced real changes -- this checks whether
those changes and everything since actually hold up). You're evaluating
whether a set of corrections, investigations, and cross-references made
since then are internally sound, honestly reported, and not quietly
overreaching -- not proposing new design work from scratch.

Where to read it
-------------------
Everything referenced here lives in the engineering atlas:
https://claude.ai/code/artifact/23feda01-9857-4508-a014-3caa61bdbd07

Specifically, revisions 37 through 43 (see Revision History at the bottom
for the exact list). In section terms: "The Layer Model" (top, diagram +
lede paragraphs), the new "City Layer" section (one card plus three
diagram-cards: two Mermaid diagrams, two hand-drawn maps, and one
composite map with a zoomed inset), the "Regional Trauma, Hazards &
Sovereignty" card (Region Layer), Design Idea 35 (City ownership wiring),
Design Idea 39 (affiliation change path), Design Idea 40 (Clan lifecycle),
Design Idea 42 (a real bug, not a proposal), the new "Country Lifecycle"
card (Faction Layer), the "Party Formation & Lifecycle" card (Group
Layer), and the "Conversation" card (Entity Action).

What changed and why
------------------------
A prior open discussion (see docs/brainstorm/layer_model_discussion_brief.txt
if you want the original, still in the repo) established Entity -> City ->
Country -> World as the real spine, with Clan and Race as cross-cutting
reach rather than containing tiers. That discussion's response flagged a
naming/scale error: "City" had actually been equated with "Region" in the
diagram (reasoning that a Region hosting ~20 buildings is city-scale), when
Region should instead be a biome/geography grouping a City sits inside, at
a scale comparable to Country and Clan rather than a synonym for City.

Direct investigation followed, and the finding was more specific than
"we mislabeled something": RegionState.kind already carries a real,
specially-handled "TOWN" value in the compiler, and a real, already-
compiled world (data/worlds/urban_political) already runs two
independently-owned TOWN-kind regions side by side -- proof that a
"City" concept already exists as data, just conflated with Region as a
class, not something needing invention from nothing. Alongside that: a
genuinely separate, already-real bug was found (state.town_center, read
by five live systems, never actually derived from region data -- Design
Idea 42) and a naming-variant trap (a real settlement tagged
type="settlement" instead of "town" silently gets none of the town
treatment).

Three visual methods were then built to make the corrected topology
concrete: Mermaid relationship diagrams, hand-drawn SVG maps (shapes and
borders, no tile grid) drawn to scale from the real urban_political
bounds, and a composite map extending that same scenario across every
layer at once (two Regions, two Countries -- one with non-contiguous
territory -- three Cities, a cross-cutting Clan, schematic Race markers),
with a zoomed inset explicitly marked as using real building/population
counts rather than invented ones.

Separately, the same lifecycle/boundary/domain test used earlier for
Entity, Clan, and Race was applied to two more layers. Group Layer came
back healthy, but the audit caught a wrong claim made earlier in Design
Idea 40 ("Group has no invite/accept step at all") -- real recruitment
turns out to go through an independent, trust-appraisal-based accept
decision (SocialAppraisalSystem.appraise_contract()), corrected in place.
Country/Faction came back partially healthy: real, live diplomatic and
territorial state transitions, but no code anywhere ever founds a new
Country or dissolves an existing one -- left as an open finding rather
than a forced design, since (unlike Clan's founding gap) nothing in the
codebase offers an analogous mechanism to reuse.

The newly-confirmed trust-appraisal mechanism was then cited back into
two other places that had previously used vaguer language for the same
idea: Design Idea 39's affiliation-switching proposal, and the
Conversation card's affection/trust gate.

What to actually check
--------------------------
1. Does the corrected topology actually hold together? Entity -> City ->
   Region ~ Country ~ Clan -> Race -> World, with City nesting inside
   Region geographically while Region/Country/Clan sit at a comparable,
   cross-cutting scale. Does this resolve the original City=Region
   conflation cleanly, or does it just relocate the same ambiguity one
   level up (e.g., is "Region ~ Country ~ Clan" doing real work, or is it
   a hedge that avoids committing to a real relationship)?

2. The composite map's central claim: a Country's territory being
   non-contiguous (two Cities in two different Regions) is presented as
   already supported by the real data model (FactionState.territory is a
   tuple, not a single value) and not requiring new design. Is that
   inference sound, or is there a load-bearing detail the map glosses
   over?

3. The real-vs-illustrative split across the three City Layer visuals.
   One diagram/map pair is drawn to scale from real, already-compiled
   world data; the other extends the same scenario into a hypothetical
   target model; the composite map's inset mixes a real building/
   population count with an illustrative surrounding political scenario.
   Does labeling carry that distinction clearly enough that a reader
   couldn't mistake the illustrative parts for verified fact, or does the
   shared visual style (same city names, same map style) blur it?

4. The Group Layer correction to Design Idea 40. The idea originally used
   "Group has no accept step, Clan needs one" as its reason for Clan
   diverging from Group's joining pattern. The correction replaces that
   with "both have an accept step; the real difference is a transactional
   expiring offer vs. a standing identity relationship." Does that revised
   reasoning actually still support the original conclusion (reuse
   Marriage's propose/accept for Clan, not Group's recruitment shape), or
   does the corrected premise actually weaken the case for treating them
   differently at all?

5. The Country Lifecycle finding. Check the reasoning, not just the
   conclusion: is "meaningful state transitions matter more than literal
   birth/death" a fair standard to apply here, or is a political actor
   that can never come into existence or cease existing missing something
   more fundamental than the finding gives it credit for? Separately: is
   "leave it open, no design proposed" the right call given there's no
   reusable precedent, or should a genuinely new mechanism have been
   sketched anyway?

6. The cross-wiring cascade. The same trust-appraisal mechanism
   (SocialAppraisalSystem.appraise_contract()) is now cited as the shared
   precedent behind three different proposals: Clan joining, affiliation
   switching, and Conversation's affection gate. Check whether these are
   actually the same shape of problem, or whether a single freshly-found
   mechanism is being overfit onto three situations that don't all need
   the same solution just because it's convenient and recently verified.

7. Whether the original discussion brief's four open questions (layer
   model's own lifecycle/boundary/domain test, the relationship-type
   taxonomy, reproduction/marriage implications, and "what doesn't fit
   any of the four link types") were actually addressed by any of this,
   or whether they're still sitting unresolved underneath the new work.

What NOT to do
-----------------
- Don't re-litigate the original City=Region correction itself -- that's
  settled and verified against real code; the question here is whether
  everything built on top of it since is sound.
- Don't propose implementing any of this -- it's still explicitly
  brainstorm-only, no code has been written for any of it.
- Don't evaluate the SVG/Mermaid markup as code quality -- treat the
  diagrams purely as communication devices; the question is whether what
  they communicate is accurate and clear, not how they're built.

How to report back
----------------------
For each of the 7 points above: does it hold up, and if not, specifically
what's shaky and why. Close with an overall judgment -- does this batch of
work (revisions 37-43) leave the layer model in a stronger, more coherent
state than the version the original discussion brief reviewed, or did
something get lost or overreached in the process of investigating and
wiring it further?
