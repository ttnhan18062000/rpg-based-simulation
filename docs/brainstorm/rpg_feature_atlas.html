Corrected in revision 3, refined in revision 4: what was first drawn as a sixth, cross-cutting "Decision" peer layer is actually four supporting domains living directly on EntityState. Action is what the entity does (combat, movement, pacing). Entity Profile is what makes it different from other entities (attributes, derived stats, class, genetics, progression). Modification is what temporarily or permanently alters its capability (wounds, status effects). Cognition is how it decides (perception, memory, strategic intelligence) — the old "Decision" grouping, renamed to match how you're thinking about it. None of the four are peers of City/Country/World; all four reason about or act on those layers while physically living on the entity.

Revision 26 — the scale hierarchy above Entity, revisited on direct request. The previous Group/Faction/Region/World labeling was accurate to the code's own class names but not to what those classes actually represent at scale. Checked directly: a RegionState hosts roughly 20 buildings in realistic test scenarios and carries population cohorts, siege state, and local economy — it already is city-scale, not a larger zone containing multiple cities. Relabeled as City rather than invented as a new layer. FactionState is genuinely Country-shaped (territory, diplomacy, military strength, tension) and, checked directly this pass, is not the fixed 4-instance system a previous pass in this conversation incorrectly claimed — see the correction note below. Clan and Race are added as two new tiers your direct request identified as missing, both confirmed genuinely absent from the codebase (see their respective cards) rather than assumed. The key structural change from before: layers are now explicitly linked rather than drawn as a flat chain — a City is contained in and owned by a Country (a real containment relationship, not just adjacency), while Clan and Race span multiple Cities/Countries via membership or population distribution rather than being owned by any single one of them.

Revision 28 — refined after open discussion, not just corrected. The real spine is Entity → City → Country → World; Clan and Race are cross-cutting dimensions with reach, not additional rungs a City or Country sits inside of — "Race is a significant force bigger than a Country" is better read as broader causal reach (a Race's population, culture, and relations can span a dozen Countries) than as literal structural size. A fourth relationship type was missing from the original three: affiliation, separate from physical containment. An entity's current City is where it is (navigation.position); its Country is who it's politically aligned with (identity.faction, a separate field, confirmed real) — walking from Country A into Country B changes location instantly but doesn't automatically change allegiance. The codebase already keeps these separate; the diagram just hadn't been making that explicit. One naming caution worth carrying forward: several of the 16 real factions (a merchant league, a cult, a warband) don't read as nation-states even though the engine treats all of them identically as territorial political actors — "Country" is the role the state plays, not necessarily the right word for every instance; "Polity" may be the more precise term going forward, worth deciding rather than assuming. And a governing principle for anything wired between layers going forward: this is not a strict precedence hierarchy (Country > Clan > Race > Entity would collapse exactly the kind of emergent tension — a character personally close to someone their Country is at war with — that makes the model worth having at all). It's a topology (the structure above) plus a set of pressures flowing bidirectionally through it, each layer answering a genuinely different question: Race answers "how does this character tend to see other races," Country answers "who is my polity's enemy," Clan answers "how do I regard this organization's members," and personal relationship answers "how do I feel about this specific person" — deliberately never collapsed into one number.

Revision 37 — City split out from Region, after direct correction and a deep investigation using concrete worked examples. The revision-26 equation of City with Region was itself wrong, not just imprecise: a Region should be a biome/geography grouping; a City is a settlement inside it, a different scale entirely, not the same class read at a different zoom level. What the investigation found, checked directly against real state classes and real world content rather than assumed: City already exists as data, just conflated with Region rather than modeled separately — RegionState.kind already carries a specially-handled "TOWN" value (see City Layer card), and a real composed world (data/worlds/urban_political) already runs two independently-owned TOWN-kind regions side by side on one map. That's proof the "one Region can border or contain multiple settlements, each independently owned" shape your correction described isn't a hypothetical future need — the content pipeline already produces exactly that, it's just never been named. What's still genuinely missing: City has no state class of its own (it borrows RegionState's biome-oriented fields wholesale), containment below City is never stored (Building has no region_id), and one specific runtime pointer that's supposed to answer "where is downtown" turned out to be silently broken for this exact reason — see Design Idea 42. The corrected topology: Entity located in City, City geographically inside Region, City owned by Country — a real chain of different relationship types, not one uniform "contains." Region, Country, and Clan then sit at a comparable, cross-cutting scale rather than one containing the others (a Country's territory can span Cities across multiple Regions; a Region can host Cities owned by different, even warring, Countries) — and Race and World are not further links in that same chain at all, reach and container respectively, which a single arrow-chain notation risks flattening back into ladder-thinking exactly like revision 26 originally did; the diagram above, not a compact sentence, is the more precise statement of the model. Region loses the political-ownership role it was implicitly given in revision 26 — owner_faction_id belongs at City scale going forward, not Region scale, which retargets Design Idea 35 accordingly.

The Core Gameplay Loop

The engine's real decision cycle, reconstructed from the strategic-cognition contract and the adventure-routing contract.

flowchart LR
    classDef stage fill:#2C6E63,stroke:#1a4740,color:#fff,font-weight:600
    P([Perceive]):::stage --> R([Remember]):::stage --> D([Decide]):::stage --> A([Act]):::stage --> W([World Reacts]):::stage --> G([Progress]):::stage --> P
Perception feeds Leads & Blockers into memory; Strategic Cognition resolves a Directive → Project → Objective chain; the resulting Action runs through Combat, Economy, or Social systems; the world updates Trauma, Demographics, and Calamity state in response; the entity accrues XP and physical consequences; perception begins again next tick.
Correction from revision 26 — a wrong claim made directly to you, corrected before it made it further than one message. Earlier in this conversation, Faction was described as "a fixed set of exactly four things" with "only one TOWN_COUNCIL for the entire world." Checked directly and that was wrong: data/content/social/factions.yaml defines 16 distinct factions today (hero_guild, town_council, wild_beast_pack, goblin_warband, merchant_league, bandit_company, forest_wardens, orc_clan, dwarven_mine_clan, moon_cult, undead_remnants, arcane_circle, swamp_tribe, dragon_cult, spirit_court, neutral), and the world compiler constructs a real, independent FactionState for every one of them, keyed by its own id. The Faction(IntEnum) 4-value type is a legacy compatibility mapping used only to name a few gold-resource pools and a handful of old string-matching branches — get_faction_enum() maps any faction string onto the nearest of the 4 legacy buckets for that narrow purpose, it does not limit how many real faction instances can exist. The real gap found this pass is different and more specific — see the Regional Trauma, Hazards & Sovereignty card, Region Layer.
Correction from revision 1: the original "Memory & Information" card conflated two genuinely separate systems — src/core/strategic.py's Leads/Blockers (knowledge about resources and problems, part of the Cognition sub-domain) and src/domains/memory/'s causal attribution & spatial memory (a distinct, Entity-layer system). They're split below. A real, previously-unreported gap also surfaced: no Buff/Debuff class exists anywhere in the engine — only level-gated skill unlocks do.
Revision 3 — mechanism-depth pass. Checked how progression, combat, equipment, genetics, and perception actually resolve, not just whether the module exists: Progression is far more flattened than the design vision's training-through-action goal — attribute points are a flat +5/level regardless of what the entity did, the attribute cap is a flat 99 from level 1 (not level-scaled), skill unlocks are 3 fixed level gates, and BreakthroughService.apply_bonuses() is a literal pass with a comment admitting it's a placeholder — there is no scroll/teaching skill-acquisition path anywhere. Combat is the opposite finding: genuinely deep (5 ordered tactical modifiers, fractional armor mitigation, per-slot durability decay, faction-aware AoE) plus a hero rebirth/permadeath system (4 generations, then permanent) that neither brainstorm mentioned. Equipment splits cleanly — the gear-scoring/auto-equip AI is real and sophisticated, but crafting is 3 hardcoded recipes and items have no rarity or class field at all, so the old bundled "Equipment, Material & Trade" card below is now three cards. Genetics is the standout: a complete, working, deterministic seed-based genetic-variation generator that zero code anywhere calls — entities are always built with a default, unpopulated AptitudeComponent(). New "Orphaned" status tier added for exactly this case. Perception held up as described: budget-capped, salience-ranked, drops zero-salience signals — real bounded attention, not decorative scoring.
Revision 4 — Entity as a network of domains. Went deliberately cross-domain this pass: Action (pacing, not just combat), Entity Profile, Modification, Cognition, checking not just whether a mechanism exists but how far a stat's influence actually reaches. Two standout results, one in each direction. Good news: Cognition Capacity (card below) is a genuinely excellent, live, stat-derived thinking/memory budget with a real cross-domain fatigue coupling to Biology — almost exactly your own example mechanism. Bad news, and a new class of finding: docs/core/attributes_and_classes.md is marked status: authoritative, authority: P0, yet documents hero classes with scaling grades, a 3-tier breakthrough chain (Warrior→Champion→Warlord), skill mastery tiers with real % bonuses, and the SPD-based action-delay formula — an entire V1 system deleted from the codebase in the "Rework RPG system" commit, months before this doc's own last_verified date. The live V2 classes/skills registries that replaced it are far flatter (4 classes, no Ranger, no scaling grades, no mastery field at all). Practically: most of what you originally sketched (training-through-action, breakthrough-gated advancement, mastery tiers) used to exist and was deliberately simplified away, not never built.
Revision 5 — reverse coverage check (per your explicit request). Every entity-relevant src/ folder and docs/ folder was listed and cross-checked against what the atlas already cited, instead of continuing to work forward from the brainstorm. Three things stood out enough to restructure around: (1) an entire, well-built Self-Model package (src/cognition/ + docs/cognition/*.md) implementing subjective self-perception — perceived weaknesses/strengths, confidence, per-context capability estimates, and a knowledge model that tracks not just facts but things the entity explicitly knows it doesn't know — live and wired, never once mentioned across 4 revisions despite self_model being one of the three fields used to define Cognition's whole scope. (2) src/engine/tactical.py's TacticalDecisionSystem — a ~770-line real-time tactical AI (target selection, cover-seeking, bracketing, guarding, kiting, anti-stalemate, skill selection) that is the actual "what do I do in this fight" layer, sitting between Strategic Intelligence's goal selection and Combat's damage math, and was completely absent. (3) a repeating pattern: beyond Genetics, three more real, well-built, typed systems were found with zero live callers — an event-driven EmotionalModel (fear/confidence/frustration/curiosity/satisfaction/panic/boredom) that directly overturns the earlier "no entity feels joy" framing, a monster-evolution service (goblin→goblin elite), and a lead-creation service superseded by inline duplicates elsewhere. Separately, core/cognition.py's declared schema turned out far larger than what's actually populated: Risk beliefs, trauma Recovery, full private-Trust/Reputation/Betrayal Relationships, and a 7-value Motivation profile (survival/reward/knowledge/loyalty/pride/curiosity/caution) are all typed and declared but have no writer anywhere in the codebase.

Suggested Build Order

Extracted every explicit “idea N” cross-reference from the cards themselves, corrected the handful where a card cited a later idea only to describe what it would unlock (not an actual dependency on it), and topologically sorted what's left. Two things this is not: a priority ranking (a Wave 3 idea can matter more than a Wave 0 one; this only says what needs what) and a claim of completeness (some connections are soft/thematic, noted in the rationale but not treated as hard blocks).
