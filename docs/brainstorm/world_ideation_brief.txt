Ideation Brief: New RPG Features, Extending the Whole World

This is a different task from the review briefs in this folder. This time:
propose NEW ideas only, across every scale of the simulation -- one
character, up through settlements, nations, peoples, and the world itself.
Do not review, critique, or suggest consistency fixes for anything that
already exists. That ground is covered elsewhere. Your job is to propose
what could exist next.

This brief has already been sent out twice. Both times, the response
reviewed this document's construction instead of answering it -- useful
feedback, already incorporated below, but not what's being asked for a
third time. Do not evaluate this brief. Do not suggest changes to it. Read
the two documents linked below, then propose actual new ideas, in the
submission format given near the end. That is the entire task.

Read these two first, in this order
---------------------------------------
1. The Unwritten World -- the creative direction:
   https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4dcbe5f7-4cea-467e-aaf0-794ee5dc7e3f
   Read this first and take it seriously. It's eight principles describing
   what this specific world is trying to be, each with real examples and an
   explicit "what failure looks like." Every idea you propose should serve
   at least one of these eight, and you should say which. This is the actual
   filter -- an idea that's "a cool RPG feature" in general but doesn't serve
   any of the eight probably belongs to a different project.

2. Simulation Capabilities -- what already exists:
   https://claude.ai/code/artifact/72b90756-e335-46ce-a2be-9ab757357bd0
   An accurate, plain-language summary of what the simulation currently
   delivers, from a single character's choices up through parties, cities,
   nations, peoples, and the living world. Three honest states: live today,
   built but switched off, or not yet built. Treat this as ground truth for
   "what already exists" -- your job is what could extend it, not to
   re-describe or re-flag what's already there.

You do not need the technical engineering atlas underneath these two --
it's real code, file paths, and verification detail, not needed for this
task. If you want it anyway: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/23feda01-9857-4508-a014-3caa61bdbd07

If either linked artifact above fails to load, stop and say so rather than
inventing or approximating its contents. Ideas that only pretend to serve
the eight principles, because the principles were never actually read, are
worse than no ideas at all. Before proposing anything, briefly confirm
that both documents were actually read -- not for ceremony, it's the
signal that the grounding step genuinely happened rather than reading the
first closely and skimming or skipping the second.

Ideas already on the table (don't duplicate these)
-------------------------------------------------------
The following are already proposed and recorded elsewhere in this project.
Don't resubmit variations on these -- build past them:

 1. A full birth event, split by species kind (natural creatures, humanoids,
    magical/demonic beings), each maturing differently.
 2. Marriage as a genuine propose/accept relationship, required for
    humanoid reproduction, restricted by species but not by role or class.
 3. A child who doesn't get a finished adult identity at birth -- a real
    childhood, then a coming-of-age choice shaped by upbringing.
 4. A second, alternate path to power alongside leveling, gated on a rare
    material a character has found and is carrying, not just experience.
 5. Genuinely divergent growth paths between two characters of the same
    class, instead of an identical numeric climb for everyone.
 6. Goblin/orc-style camps that actually grow, raid outward, and can be
    found and cleared by an adventurer.
 7. A settlement-capacity distinction separating city-building peoples,
    camp-building peoples, and peoples that never build anything at all --
    independent of raw intelligence.
 8. Leaderless, spreading populations (think spider nests, slime colonies)
    as their own kind of place, distinct from a led settlement -- nobody
    plans a raid, but the infestation still spreads if ignored.
 9. A single dangerous creature's own claimed territory, defined by and
    tied to that specific occupant -- meaningfully different once its
    occupant is gone.
10. Places that change identity over time as a record of what actually
    happened to them -- a city destroyed in a siege becoming a ruin, an
    abandoned camp later occupied by something else entirely.
11. Wiring exploration to crafting through rare materials tied to specific
    places, so a specific ruin or biome becomes a real reason to go there.
12. A nation that can actually be founded or wiped out during play, instead
    of the set of nations being permanently fixed from world creation.
13. A real "grow" option for a strong, confident nation -- today a nation
    already reconsiders its priorities every moment, but has no path to
    proactively expand, only to defend, trade, or commission quests.
14. A nation's own population pressure genuinely driving that expansion,
    and driving new camps or nests to form in unclaimed land nearby.
15. Organizations spanning more than one city or nation -- a guild, a
    bloodline, a trading company -- with real membership and real assets,
    independent of whoever currently rules the land its members stand on.
16. A whole people's collective population and standing, not just one
    character's personal race -- is a species growing or shrinking, and how
    does one people generally regard another, as a baseline under any one
    individual's personal feelings.
17. A real religion and pantheon system -- today there is a single unused
    word suggesting one might exist, and nothing else.
18. Turning on the world's already-built system for organically generating
    rumors and quest hooks from what's actually happening, instead of every
    quest coming from a fixed source.
19. A meaningfully deeper crafting content library -- the gating system is
    real, there just isn't much to make yet.
20. Letting a character directly hand an item to, or team up with, another
    character through a real request either side can refuse -- both are
    currently automatic or don't exist.
21. A child starting with an inherited echo of their parents' public
    reputation -- trusted a little faster, or judged a little harder,
    before they've done anything themselves.
22. An organization's own reputation diverging from any single member's --
    one member's betrayal casting suspicion on every other member, who
    then has to actively distinguish themselves from their own group.
23. A grudge that outlives the person who started it, inherited by their
    lineage the way a name is -- including the possibility of two people
    falling for each other across a feud that belongs to their parents,
    not to them.
24. An isolated settlement's felt loyalty to its distant capital eroding
    purely through cultural drift over time, with no single event, vote,
    or rebellion causing it.
25. The world's own recorded history feeding back into behavior -- a
    hero's fame, once it's real enough, inspiring other characters who
    never met them to become adventurers themselves.
26. A dying character's last spoken intention becoming a real, lasting
    pull on their heir's motivations -- distinct from inherited traits or
    inherited possessions.

An idea is not new merely because it adds another subsystem, modifier,
tier, resource, or consequence on top of one of the twenty-six above -- "camp
diplomacy," "camp reputation," or "camp upgrade tiers" are all still just
idea 6 wearing a new hat. To count as new, it has to introduce a genuinely
different source of emergent behavior, not decorate an existing one.

There's a related, sharper line worth drawing explicitly: building on an
existing gap in a new direction is allowed; proposing to simply fill that
gap is not. If conversation between characters is already a known missing
piece, "build conversation" is a duplicate -- but "a character deliberately
spreads false information through conversation to manipulate a rival
settlement" is a genuinely new idea that happens to depend on conversation
existing. The difference is whether the proposal is the gap itself, or
something new that would only be possible once the gap is closed.

Also avoid ideas whose main value is added content volume -- more classes,
more monsters, more recipes, more building types -- unless that new
content creates a qualitatively different world behavior, not just more
of the same behavior. "Seasonal monsters," "more occupations," "more
dungeon types," and "more faction archetypes" are all still content
expansion dressed as ideation unless something about them changes what
the world can actually do.

What makes a good submission
---------------------------------
- Genuinely new: not a rewording of the twenty-six ideas above, and not just
  restating something Simulation Capabilities already flags as missing or
  inactive as if it were a fresh proposal.
- Serves the direction: name which of the eight Unwritten World principles
  it serves, and how. An idea that doesn't clearly serve any of them needs
  its own stated reason for being worth considering anyway.
- Grounded, not disconnected: something that could plausibly extend what
  Simulation Capabilities describes, at any scale -- one character, a
  settlement, a nation, a whole people, or the world itself. You don't need
  to know how it would be built, only that it makes sense given what
  already exists and what already doesn't.
- Plain and standalone: no code, file paths, or technical implementation
  detail. Describe it the way you'd describe it to someone who will never
  open the source code.
- Creates a new possible story, not just a new possible number: the
  sharpest version of principle 1 (no one hands down the story) is asking,
  for every idea, what can now happen in the world that couldn't happen
  before. "Cities get a prosperity rating" is a stat. "A prosperous
  frontier city starts pulling migrants away from struggling settlements
  nearby, quietly shifting political power in the process" is a story
  shape nothing else on this list currently produces. Prefer the second
  kind every time.

Don't force coverage across every scale for its own sake. The list above
mentions one character up through the whole world so you know the full
range is genuinely open -- not as a checklist to fill in. If the strongest
ideas cluster at two or three scales, that's a better outcome than
inventing weaker ideas just to cover the rest. The best ideas will often
start at one scale and ripple into others on their own -- a single
character's decision that eventually reshapes a Clan, a City, and a
Country is exactly the kind of cross-scale effect worth finding, not
something to avoid because it doesn't fit neatly in one row.

Submission format
---------------------
One idea per entry:
- A short name for the idea.
- A paragraph describing what it is and what it would change.
- Which scale it mainly touches: one character, a settlement (city, camp,
  nest, lair, or site), the wider land, a nation, an organization spanning
  borders, a whole people, or the world itself. An idea can touch more than
  one -- say so if it does, including if it starts at one scale and ripples
  into others.
- Which of the eight Unwritten World principles it serves, and one sentence
  on how.
- Why it isn't a duplicate of anything in the twenty-six already on the table,
  or of a gap Simulation Capabilities already names -- one sentence, doing
  your own check rather than leaving it to be caught later.

No fixed number of ideas expected -- quality over quantity. A handful of
genuinely new, well-considered directions is worth more than a long list of
minor variations on what's already on the table above.
