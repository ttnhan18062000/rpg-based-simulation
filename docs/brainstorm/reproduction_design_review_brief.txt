Review Brief: Reproduction, Marriage & Coming-of-Age Design
================================================================

This is a review of a NEW, not-yet-implemented design proposal for a
fantasy RPG simulation engine -- three connected mechanics: how characters
are born, how marriage works, and how a child becomes an adult. None of
this exists in the simulation yet. You're not checking it against reality
(there's no implementation to check against) -- you're stress-testing
whether the design itself is sound, complete, and internally consistent.

Where to read it
-------------------
The full proposal is written up as three entries (numbered 32, 33, 34) in
the "Design Ideas & Extensions" section of the engineering atlas:
https://claude.ai/code/artifact/23feda01-9857-4508-a014-3caa61bdbd07

Read entries 32-34 specifically. The rest of the atlas is there for context
if you need to understand what a referenced existing system actually does
(the entries cite real, already-built mechanisms this design reuses), but
you don't need to read the whole thing end to end.

The design in brief, so you know what you're evaluating
------------------------------------------------------------
- Reproduction splits into three paths by species classification: natural
  creatures reproduce fast via a spawn-camp pattern with no tracked
  parents; humans/humanoids reproduce through a real, gated event requiring
  marriage, both parents adult and alive, proximity, and a cooldown;
  magical/demonic beings are born through non-biological triggers (a
  ritual, a calamity, a corrupted location) instead of a parent pair.
- Marriage is a new durable relationship state, entered through a genuine
  propose/accept exchange -- explicitly the first such handshake anywhere
  in the simulation's design. It's the actual prerequisite for human/
  humanoid reproduction.
- Coming of age: a naturally-born child doesn't get a class/archetype
  assigned at birth. It's deferred until they transition from a Child life
  stage to Adult, at which point the choice is made -- weighted by
  personality, parental occupation, and regional need, not hand-scripted.

The stated design goals to check this against
--------------------------------------------------
The person driving this project has been explicit about the target: realistic
enough to feel meaningful, but NOT simulated in exhausting biological detail
-- no gestation tracking, no fertility stats, no courtship AI. The one hard
requirement stated directly: "a child should not [be] born at age 18
immediately" -- there must be a real growth period, not instant adulthood.
Keep this balance in mind throughout: flagging "this doesn't model X realistic
detail" is only a useful note if X is something the stated goals actually
care about, not a generic realism checklist.

What to actually check
-----------------------
1. Internal consistency across the three entries. Do the requirements for
   birth actually line up with what marriage promises to provide? Does the
   coming-of-age mechanic's timing make sense given how Life Stage
   transitions are described?
2. Completeness of the "requirements" for the gated birth event. Is the
   listed requirement set (both parents alive, adult, married, same
   location, cooldown) sufficient to prevent obviously broken outcomes
   (e.g., runaway population growth, population collapse, orphaned logic
   states), or is something load-bearing missing?
3. Whether the three species paths (natural / human / magical) are
   meaningfully differentiated or just relabeled versions of each other.
4. The coming-of-age weighting (personality, parental occupation, regional
   need) -- does this plausibly produce interesting variety, or would it
   converge on predictable, boring outcomes in practice (e.g., every child
   in a Guard-heavy region becomes a Guard)?
5. Edge cases the design doesn't address: What happens to a child if both
   parents die before coming-of-age? Can a married pair have unlimited
   children, or is there an implied cap? Does marriage have any purpose
   beyond gating reproduction, or is it purely a means to an end?
6. Whether this design leans too far into "too much realism" or not far
   enough, given the explicit balance requested above.

What NOT to do
---------------
- Don't check this against the actual simulation codebase -- there's
  nothing to check, this hasn't been built. Your job is evaluating the
  design's own internal logic and completeness.
- Don't suggest simulating pregnancy, fertility, or courtship in more
  detail -- that's explicitly the wrong direction per the stated goals.
- Don't propose an entirely different reproduction system from scratch --
  the direction is set; the job is to find gaps, inconsistencies, and
  missing edge cases in this specific proposal, not to redesign it.

How to report back
-------------------
For each issue: which of the three entries it's in (32/33/34), what's
inconsistent, incomplete, or unaddressed, and a suggested resolution if you
have one. Close with a judgment: does this proposal hang together as a
coherent design ready to be scoped into real tickets, or does it need
another brainstorming pass first -- and if so, on what specifically.
