Ideation Brief: New Ideas for Character Design
================================================

This is a different task from the last review. Last time, an agent checked
the Character Capabilities document for internal consistency, clarity, and
accuracy. That's done. This time: propose NEW ideas only. Do not review,
critique, or suggest wording/consistency fixes for the existing document --
that ground has already been covered.

What you're building on
------------------------
Character Capabilities, published here:
https://claude.ai/code/artifact/01a48398-d8e1-4f45-b4b2-ed1dde0672fd

Read it first. It's an accurate, verified, non-technical summary of what a
fantasy RPG simulation engine currently delivers for individual characters --
what's live, what's built but inactive, and what doesn't exist yet. Treat
everything in it as ground truth for "what already exists." Your job is to
propose what could exist next, not to re-describe or re-flag what's already
there.

Ideas already on the table (don't duplicate these)
-----------------------------------------------------
The following directions have already been proposed and recorded elsewhere
in this project. Don't resubmit variations on these -- build past them:

 1. Connect already-built-but-disconnected systems before building new ones
    (several real systems exist fully built and just aren't switched on or
    wired up).
 2. Make action speed actually vary by character instead of being identical
    for everyone.
 3. Finish the unfinished practice/mastery progression system.
 4. Unify the scattered temporary-condition mechanisms into one real system.
 5. Let a character's own self-assessment of a fight influence whether they
    commit to it.
 6. Build a teaching/scroll-based skill-learning system on top of the trust
    relationships that already exist, rather than inventing new state.
 7. Extend grudge/rival relationships so they persist across major story
    breaks, not just within one continuous stretch of play.
 8. Either finish or remove a second, currently-empty inner-mental-life
    system that duplicates ideas already covered elsewhere.
 9. Deliberately decide, one by one, whether each currently-inactive system
    should be turned on or abandoned, rather than leaving it undecided.
10. Make the existing (but never-firing) inheritance system actually able to
    fire.
11. Build a real branching character-build system, reusing existing
    milestone and transformation mechanics rather than starting from zero.
12. Route contradiction-detection (catching when a trusted source turns out
    to be wrong) through an already-live system instead of the switched-off
    one.
13. Require the same kind of earned trust/affection that already gates some
    interactions to also gate trading and forming a party together, which
    don't exist yet.
14. Add a species classification layer (an intelligence tier, plus a
    natural/magical/demonic-style category) that determines which characters
    get an open-ended, self-directed life path versus a simpler one.

The design vision to aim at
-----------------------------
This is the actual goal behind the whole project -- ideas should serve this,
not just be interesting in isolation:

A simulation with genuinely complex, diverse life paths for characters, and
enough different kinds of characters interacting with each other to produce
a believable, lived-in fantasy world. Concretely, this has come up as:

- Humans split into functionally different life paths: some characters are
  "adventurers" who effectively discover their own path through the world
  (open-ended, self-directed), while others -- ordinary townspeople -- follow
  a more fixed, predictable life. That split should generalize: how much of
  an open-ended life path a character gets should scale with how intelligent
  they are, not just their species.
- On top of intelligence, a broader classification (natural creature, magical
  being, demonic entity, or other typical fantasy categories) should shape
  what's mechanically available or different for that kind of character.
- Low-intelligence or monster-like characters should still have a real life
  path, just a simpler one -- likely growing stronger through direct means
  like combat or consumption rather than structured training, and
  reproducing or replacing themselves faster than more complex characters
  do.
- Above all: many different kinds of characters, with real differences in
  how they live and grow, actually interacting with each other -- fighting,
  trading, forming bonds, competing, cooperating -- is what makes the
  simulated world feel real.

What makes a good submission
-------------------------------
- Genuinely new: not a rewording of the 14 ideas above, and not just
  restating a known gap from the Character Capabilities document as if it
  were a proposal.
- Grounded: something that could plausibly extend what's described in
  Character Capabilities, not disconnected from how the simulation already
  works. You don't need to know how it would be built -- just that it makes
  sense given what already exists.
- In service of the vision above, or a clear, stated reason why it's a
  worthwhile idea even if it isn't.
- Written in the same plain, standalone style as Character Capabilities --
  no code, file paths, or technical implementation details. Describe the
  idea the way you'd describe it to someone who will never open the source
  code.

Submission format
-------------------
One idea per entry:
- A short name for the idea.
- A paragraph describing what it is and what it would change about how
  characters behave or grow.
- Which part of a character it mainly touches: what they do, who they are,
  how injury/conditions affect them, or how they think and remember.
- One sentence on why it serves the design vision above (or why it's worth
  considering even if it doesn't).

No fixed number of ideas expected -- quality over quantity. A handful of
genuinely new, well-considered directions is worth more than a long list of
minor variations.
