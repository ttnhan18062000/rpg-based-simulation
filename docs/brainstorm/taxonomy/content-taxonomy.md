# RPG Content Taxonomy and Cardinality Model

Status: repository investigation snapshot  
Snapshot date: 2026-09-03  
Scope: implemented content plus planned and brainstormed RPG-core features  
Purpose: content inventory and quantity control; this document makes no art-production decisions

## Method and counting boundary

This report treats the repository as the source of truth and uses four evidence layers:

1. The live content catalog under `data/content/`, loaded through
   `src/content/repository.py::CatalogRepository` and its `CANONICAL_FAMILIES` list.
2. Runtime registries and typed gameplay vocabularies under `src/`, especially hard-coded definitions
   that do not yet have catalog equivalents.
3. World modules, compositions, and scenario files, which are loaded separately from the canonical
   catalog.
4. Approved plans and non-approved brainstorms under `docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/` and
   `docs/brainstorm/`. Planned counts are never included in current counts.

The current catalog baseline comes from `config/content_inventory.json`, generated by
`tools/generate_content_inventory.py`. Direct recounts were used where that inventory intentionally does
not descend into embedded definitions, notably the 40 `quest_definitions` records inside 16 of the 20
world modules.

Counts in this report mean:

- **Authored identity**: a definition with its own ID and a meaningful gameplay or semantic role.
- **Profile**: reusable configuration assembled into other identities; it is counted as content but not as
  another actor/item identity.
- **Parameterized variant**: one identity with changed values, rank, duration, stack, or quantity; normally
  not counted separately.
- **Generated/runtime instance**: a spawned entity, item stack, quest opportunity, place, event, or world
  created from definitions; never added to the authored count merely because many instances exist.
- **Planned type**: a proposed schema or category with no current concrete definition. It is shown as zero
  current and is projected only where plans provide a defensible bound.

## 1. Executive Summary

The repository has six major content families: actors and living definitions; items/economy; abilities,
progression, and conditions; world geography and objects; social/institutional content; and quests/world
assembly. The generated inventory contains **552 records across 34 non-generated categories**; this number
excludes the two generated world-composition snapshots reported in a thirty-fifth category. A current direct
recount adds **40 embedded quest blueprints**, producing **592 authored/configuration records** before
compatibility records and hard-coded registries are added. The 592 total also excludes the two generated
snapshots.
That is not a count of unique player-facing identities: profiles, metadata axes, relationship edges, and
assembly recipes are deliberately separate records. A practical current identity-bearing baseline is
approximately **200–240 definitions**, depending on whether roles, terrain, services, and biome definitions
approximately **200–240 definitions**, depending chiefly on whether roles and terrain definitions are
treated as independently recognizable gameplay identities; the reproducible lower lens below includes
services and biomes.

The lower end is reproducible as 203 definitions: 29 archetypes + 36 items + 4 executable skills + 3
breakthroughs + 13 races + 16 factions + 20 runtime regions + 13 biomes + 12 resources + 9 buildings + 8
services + 40 quest blueprints. Adding the 23 role identities and 10 terrain identities gives 236. This
range is a lens over overlapping catalogs, not another canonical inventory total.

There is no defensible all-surface, deduplicated grand total today: compatibility aliases, overlapping
material/item records, embedded definitions, hard-coded registries, profiles, and runtime-generated state
use different identity rules. The 552, 592, and 200–240 figures answer three different inventory questions
and should not be added together.

The expected scale is moderate for individually authored actors and items, but large for relationship
edges and generated narrative state. The largest expected cardinalities are:

1. **Race-relation edges**: 0 current; up to 156 directed pairs for 13 races.
2. **Faction-relation edges**: 75 current directed records covering 41 unordered faction pairs; hard
   current-roster ceilings are 240 directed non-self edges or 120 unordered pairs.
3. **Quest content**: 40 authored blueprints plus potentially unbounded runtime opportunities and instances.
4. **Actor archetypes**: 29 current; conservatively 40–60 as roles/species receive broader coverage.
5. **Items**: 36 identities now; conservatively 50–80 if place-tied material and personal-item systems
   receive content.

Recipes are the next large authored family at 25 current and a low-confidence 35–60 expected.

The largest uncertainty is not raw catalog size but **activation and representation drift**. The catalog has
16 unique skill labels but only four executable `SkillDefinition` records. It has 25 recipe records but the
live `src/core/recipes.py::RecipeRegistry` still contains three. It has trinket/tool tags that the core item
bootstrap maps to `MATERIAL` unless another recognized category is present. Counts therefore distinguish
"authored in data" from "independently executable at runtime."

Planned RPG features mostly add durable relationships and generated instances—Households, Clans,
marriages, births, Places, histories, local reputations, beliefs—not large fixed catalogs. Their upper bound
is usually per-world population or relationship combinatorics, not a finite list of authored definitions.

## 4. Detailed Category Analysis

### 4.1 Actors, species, roles, classes, and archetypes

The canonical actor identity is the **entity archetype**, not a race, role, stat profile, or spawned entity.
`data/content/entities/entity_archetypes.yaml` contains 29 archetypes. Each composes references to one race,
faction, role, stat profile, combat profile, cognition profile, drive profile, inventory profile, optional
skill profile, traits, and themes. `src/content/schema.py::EntityArchetypeDefinition` defines this shape, and
`src/content/resolver.py` resolves those references before world compilation.

The 29 archetypes span 12 of the 13 race definitions. Their distribution is uneven: 11 human, four
goblin, two wolf, two elf, two orc, two lizardfolk, and one each for spider, undead, spirit, dragonkin,
troll, and dwarf. Slime has a race definition but no archetype. Eighteen of 23 roles appear in archetypes;
scout has four entries, leader and mage three each, while many roles have one. This is why a practical
40–60 archetype projection is more defensible than multiplying all 13 races by all 23 roles: compatibility
rules intentionally prohibit many combinations, and `docs/audits/D07_content_depth.md` recommends closing
distribution gaps rather than filling the Cartesian product.

`data/content/living/races.yaml` provides 13 race/species definitions. These compose eight body models, six
need profiles, six sense profiles, seven cognition profiles, seven drive profiles, natural traits, attribute
tendencies, and compatible roles. These are 34 reusable configuration profiles, not additional actor
identities. M2 plans add species classification and a race-relations registry; M3 adds species lifecycle and
reproduction behavior. Neither plan names new species, so 13–20 is intentionally conservative and low
confidence.

Roles and classes are separate axes:

- `data/content/social/roles.yaml` contains 23 occupational/ecological roles. The content adapter maps them
  to six legacy `EntityRole` buckets from `src/core/enums.py`.
- `src/core/classes.py::CLASS_REGISTRY` contains four character classes: NOVICE, WARRIOR, MAGE, and ROGUE.
- `data/content/spawn_tables.yaml::default_class_table` assigns those classes to only six legacy role keys.

M1 made occupation change reachable, while M3 plans coming-of-age branch selection. Those mechanisms can
create many life paths from four classes and 23 roles without creating new authored class identities.

`data/content/entities/populations.yaml` contains 14 population templates. Counts inside their `members`
maps create entity instances; for example, a four-wolf pack still contributes only the referenced wolf
archetypes to the identity count. World-module `population_recipes` can parameterize counts, so population
instance quantity is bounded by world budgets, not by template count.

The compatibility file `data/content/compatibility/legacy_enemy_projection.yaml` maps six legacy enemy IDs
onto six existing archetypes. `src/core/registries.py::ArchetypeToEnemyRegistryAdapter` builds
`EnemyRegistry` from those projections. These are compatibility aliases, not a second enemy taxonomy.
The content catalog intentionally has no universal `enemy` flag; hostility is contextual through faction,
relationships, and perspective.

### 4.2 Items, equipment, resources, recipes, and services

`data/content/world/items.yaml` contains 36 item definitions. `src/content/schema.py::ItemDefinition`
stores open category tags, rarity, value, materials, optional use kind, class fit, and equipment slot. The
current authored split is:

| Item group | Count | IDs / basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Weapons | 10 | `weapon` tag |
| Armor/shields | 2 | `armor` tag |
| Trinkets | 2 | `trinket` tag |
| Consumables | 2 | `consumable` tag |
| Tools | 2 | `tool` tag |
| Materials | 18 | `material` tag; same identity set as foundation materials |

The groups are currently mutually exclusive at the primary-category level, though secondary tags overlap
(for example, magic, arcane, healing, fire, and anti-undead). Weapon functional tags identify five melee,
two magic, and one ranged weapon; the ember axe and venom dagger are weapons tagged by element/effect
rather than a common family. There is no authoritative `WeaponFamily` enum, so naming-derived families such
as sword, staff, bow, axe, and dagger should be treated as an analysis convenience, not schema truth.

The catalog uses three rarity values: 13 COMMON, 12 UNCOMMON, and 11 RARE. Rarity is metadata on a base
item definition. The engine does not generate a common/uncommon/rare copy of every base item, so rarity
must not multiply 36 into 108.

`src/core/models/inventory.py::ItemKind` exposes MATERIAL, CONSUMABLE, WEAPON, ARMOR, and CURRENCY.
`src/core/items.py::ItemRegistry.bootstrap` recognizes category tags for only those kinds. A trinket or
tool without another recognized category falls back to MATERIAL. This is a behavior/representation gap,
not evidence that trinkets and tools are absent from authored content.

Equipment has five engine slots—HEAD, TORSO, LEGS, MAIN_HAND, OFF_HAND—but catalog bootstrap currently
places weapons in MAIN_HAND, ordinary armor in TORSO, and shields in OFF_HAND. There are no current head or
leg armor identities. Body-model content has a separate free-form vocabulary (`weapon`, `armor`, `tool`,
`trinket`), so body slots describe biological capability rather than five concrete equipment positions.

`data/content/world/resources.yaml` defines 12 harvestable resource-node types. Each points to an item or
material identity and adds harvesting duration, charge, biome, tool, and runtime mapping metadata. Spawned
`ResourceNodeState` objects are runtime instances and do not add identities.

Crafting has an important dual representation:

- `data/content/world/recipes.yaml` has 25 authored recipe records, used by catalog adapters and services.
- `src/core/recipes.py::RecipeRegistry` has three hard-coded recipes: iron sword, iron shield, and health
  potion.

The 25 catalog recipes do not imply 25 unique result identities: several alternate transformations output
small potions, rations, warding charms, silvered blades, or iron swords. Recipe identity is the authored
transformation, not merely its output item. M4 Ideas 49/50 would activate place-tied materials and material-gated
progression; most referenced materials and recipes already exist, so the plan initially increases
connectivity more than catalog count.

Eight service definitions in `data/content/world/services.yaml` describe trade, crafting, rest, healing,
security, resource access, spiritual, and magic services. Nine building definitions host services, but a
service and a building remain separate content identities because one describes an affordance and the other
an object type.

### 4.3 Skills, progression, effects, and conditions

`src/core/skills.py::SKILL_REGISTRY` contains four executable skills: power_strike, HEAVY_STRIKE,
fireball, and swift_reflexes. Three are ACTIVE and one is PASSIVE; categories are PHYSICAL or MAGICAL even
though the `SkillCategory` enum also permits ELEMENTAL.

`data/content/entities/skill_profiles.yaml` contains ten loadout profiles with 16 unique string labels:
aimed_shot, ambush, appraise, backstab, bargain, dirty_strike, firebolt, forge_basic, hold_line, pounce,
power_strike, relentless_advance, repair, shield_bash, track_beast, and track_scent. Only power_strike has an
exact executable registry match. `firebolt` is not `fireball`. These labels are authored loadout content,
but most are not independently executable `SkillDefinition` identities. The stable count should therefore
report **4 executable skills / 16 referenced labels**, not choose one number and hide the mismatch.

`src/progression/leveling.py::LevelingService` has a level cap of 99 and three unlock thresholds (levels 2,
5, and 10). Levels, XP values, AP totals, and numeric attribute allocations are parameterized state, not 99
content identities. `src/progression/breakthroughs.py::BreakthroughService.REGISTRY` contains three authored
breakthroughs: iron_will, fleet_foot, and titan_grip. Those do count individually because they have IDs,
names, and distinct effects.

The catalog declares 15 attribute axes in `data/content/foundation/attributes.yaml`. Runtime progression
uses `AttributeComponent` and legacy formulas that do not cleanly expose the same vocabulary. Attribute
values are numeric dimensions, never separate content identities.

There is no unified status-effect registry. The current condition-like systems are bespoke:

- `src/core/state.py::WoundState` stores a generated ID, free-form kind, severity, and penalties.
- `src/engine/rpg_depth.py::WoundService.create_wound` currently generates CRUSH, SLASH, or PIERCE by
  severity; the state comment also documents BURN as an allowed string, but no live constructor branch
  generates it.
- `src/core/state.py::ScarState` is a persistent generated record derived from a wound.
- `BiologicalComponent.well_rested_until` is a timed marker, not a status definition.
- `swift_reflexes` is a passive skill, not an instance of a generic buff type.

Duration, severity, stacks, and penalty values therefore do not multiply the effect count. Planned temporal
modifier definitions may eventually create a reusable condition/effect dimension, but the proposal does not
approve a concrete registry or roster.

### 4.4 World geography, objects, and the planned Place model

The current authored world hierarchy is flat:

- 10 terrain definitions in `data/content/world/terrain.yaml` provide movement and hazard multipliers.
- 13 biome definitions in `data/content/world/biomes.yaml` compose terrain, materials, factions, themes,
  and danger.
- 20 runtime-region definitions in `data/content/world/runtime_regions.yaml` name adventure destinations.
- 9 ecology definitions combine biomes, factions, populations, resources, services, and relationship
  models.
- 9 building types and 12 resource-node types create runtime objects.

World modules author additional region instances and recipes; their IDs often overlap catalog concepts.
These are references/placements, not automatically new global region types. `src/worldbuilding/schema.py`
defines `RegionSpec`, `ResourceNodeSpec`, and `BuildingSpec`, while `src/worldbuilding/compiler.py` creates
runtime state. Runtime `GroundItemState`, `CorpseState`, `ChestState`, `CampState`, and `LocalScarState` in
`src/core/state.py` are instance schemas. The repository has no authored chest, corpse, or local-scar
catalog.

Idea 66 is approved in `docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/rpg_design_roadmap.md` (M2 and Sequencing Rules) but
not implemented. `docs/brainstorm/rpg_expected_schemas.html::Region & Place` proposes Region as a territory
containing `PlaceState` records with seven `PlaceKind` values: CITY, CAMP, NEST, LAIR, RUIN, DUNGEON, and
LANDMARK. These seven values are categories, not seven concrete Places.
Existing region/module identities such as hometown, goblin camp, old mine, haunted battlefield, and sacred
grove are likely migration inputs. A reasonable initial authored Place range is 20–50, while runtime-created
camps, nests, and lairs could scale with world history.

Place transformations do not create a new identity each time a Place changes kind. A CITY becoming a RUIN
is normally one persistent Place with a changed state and history, unless the future implementation assigns
a new ID by explicit design.

### 4.5 Social, faction, relationship, Clan, and Household content

`data/content/social/factions.yaml` contains 16 faction identities. The four-value
`src/core/enums.py::Faction` enum is a legacy behavior bucket, not the authored roster. Similarly, the 75
records in `data/content/social/faction_relationships.yaml` are directed relationship content between
factions, not 75 more organizations.

With 16 factions there are 240 possible directed non-self edges and 120 possible unordered pairs. The 75
authored directed records cover **41 unique unordered pairs**: 34 pairs have both directions authored and
seven have only one direction. `docs/audits/D07_content_depth.md` describes 75/120 as 62.5% coverage, but
that mixes directed records with an unordered denominator; actual unordered pair coverage is 41/120
(34.2%). This report retains all three metrics so future counts do not repeat that ambiguity.

The 12 relationship axes—hostility, trust, fear, respect, territorial conflict, trade affinity, resource
competition, religious conflict, ancient grudge, kinship, dominance, and debt—are shared metadata. An edge
with four populated axes is one relationship record, not four identities.

`data/content/social/perspectives.yaml` contains six faction-centered perspective profiles. These project
other factions into contextual labels and should not be counted as factions or relation edges.

At entity level, `src/core/models/social.py::RelationshipRole` contains NEUTRAL, FRIEND, and RIVAL. Runtime
`SocialBond` records scale with ordered entity pairs. M3's marriage/dependency work in
`docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/rpg_m3_family_species_epic.md` and the Household portfolio in
`docs/brainstorm/codex/2026-08-27-core-rpg-new-idea-portfolio.md::Household continuity` imply spouse,
parent/child, dependent/guardian, mentor/apprentice, or similar semantic roles, but no final unified roster
is approved. The expected 5–10 range is consequently medium-confidence for mechanism scale and
low-confidence for names.

`src/core/strategic.py::ContractKind` contains seven kinds: RECRUITMENT, LOAN, PROTECTION, MERCHANT,
POSITION_SWAP, TEAM_UP, and PAID_INFORMATION. Contracts are runtime obligations. The new-idea portfolio's
promises, favors, debts, and oaths may extend this vocabulary, but every agreement instance is not authored
content.

Clans and Households must remain distinct:

- M2/M4 Ideas 36/40 in `docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/rpg_m2_foundational_systems_epic.md` and
  `docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/rpg_m4_beyond_city_epic.md` propose `ClanState`, a multi-location
  membership organization with leadership and a lifecycle. `orc_clan` and `dwarven_mine_clan` are current
  faction names only; they are not current Clan instances.
- The accepted but not roadmap-approved Household proposal in
  `docs/brainstorm/codex/2026-08-27-core-rpg-new-idea-portfolio.md::Household continuity` connects family,
  home, resources, care, succession, and memory. It proposes generated durable households, not a fixed
  authored roster.

M2 Idea 37 in `docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/rpg_m2_foundational_systems_epic.md` proposes a race-relations
registry matching the qualitative faction-relation record shape. For 13 races its hard ceiling is 13 × 12
= 156 directed edges. A sparse registry with a default is expected; 156 is a capacity bound, not a
recommended authoring target.

### 4.6 Quests, goals, objectives, events, modules, and scenarios

`src/worldbuilding/schema.py::QuestDefinition` defines six authored blueprint types: escort, hunt, fetch,
explore, defend, and investigate. A direct count finds 40 blueprint IDs across 16 of 20 world modules:
eight hunt, eight investigate, seven fetch, six escort, six defend, and five explore. These are distinct
authored quest identities.

`src/core/models/quests.py::QuestKind` has a different six-value runtime vocabulary: HUNT, GATHER, EXPLORE,
LIBERATE, BOUNTY, ESCORT. This is a representation boundary, not 12 quest types. Future counting should
report blueprint type and runtime quest kind separately until an explicit mapping converges them.

`QuestOpportunity` and world-emergence `QuestSeed` records are generated from pressures and events. The same
blueprint can create multiple runtime quests, and a dynamic opportunity can exist without a static blueprint.
Runtime quest counts are therefore per-run metrics, potentially unbounded across an indefinitely running
world.

Decision vocabularies are metadata for generated behavior:

- 14 `GoalKind` values registered with goal scorers.
- 14 `ProjectKind` values.
- 14 `ObjectiveKind` values.
- 16 `RouteFamily` values in `src/domains/adventure/schema.py`.
- 28 `WorldEventCategory` values in `src/domains/world_emergence/schema.py`.

These types can create many runtime goal, project, objective, route-option, and event records, but their
instances are not separately authored content.

World assembly has 20 modules across seven types: six conflict, four settlement, three ecology, two
terrain, two population, two danger-zone, and one economy. There are seven authored compositions, two
checked-in generated compositions, and 14 simulation scenario definitions. Module parameters and procedural
generation create many valid worlds from these bases; the checked-in generated files are snapshots, not new
authored composition families.

### 4.7 Planned life-path, history, and temporal content

The M2–M6 plans are intended to add substantial gameplay state but comparatively few finite authored
rosters. The authority for this status is `docs/plans/rpg_design_roadmap/rpg_design_roadmap.md` and its
`rpg_m2_*` through `rpg_m6_*` milestone documents; brainstorm-only extensions are identified separately:

- M2: species classification, population seeding, Clan shape, race relations, cognition and interaction
  activation, ownership, and Place transitions.
- M3: reproduction, population feedback, dependents, coming of age, marriage, and species lifecycle timing.
- M4: settlement capacity/personality, Camp/Nest/Lair behavior, material ambition, expansion, Clan
  lifecycle, information hubs, and economic vacancies.
- M5: local/inherited reputation, inherited feud, dying wish, Chronicle feedback, misremembering, and
  emergent belief.
- M6: affiliation change, loyalty drift, home/Place attachment, exile, return, and refugees.

The accepted new-idea portfolio in
`docs/brainstorm/codex/2026-08-27-core-rpg-new-idea-portfolio.md` adds Households, richer commitments,
witnessed testimony, and meaningful failure as independent candidates; "accepted" here means accepted by
that brainstorm's own disposition, not approved roadmap scope. Most other ideas fold into planned systems.
The non-authorizing temporal proposal at
`docs/brainstorm/codex/2026-08-28-core-rpg-temporal-axis-proposal.md` adds configuration categories for a
calendar profile, activity definitions, temporal modifiers, species lifecycle profiles, and routine
profiles. None has current concrete entries, and the proposal explicitly leaves several numeric and roster
decisions open. They are therefore shown as future taxonomy nodes rather than assigned invented counts.

History-like records—turning points, Chronicle entries, memories, testimonies, beliefs, anniversaries,
reputation observations—scale with events and entities. Their **type vocabularies** can stay small while
their **runtime record cardinality** becomes very large. Retention bounds and summarization policies, rather
than an authored-content cap, will control them.

The repository does not currently implement an affix/enchantment generator or a base item × material ×
rarity product. Future counting must not assume one merely because items carry material and rarity fields.
Idea 30's significant-item instance layer would instead make a **small selected subset** of runtime items
persistent individual identities with provenance.

## 8. Expected Maximum Content Model

Categories are placed by the upper end of their practical mature range, not only by today's count. A
category whose present roster is small but whose supported expansion range crosses 30 is therefore listed
as large. Structural ceilings are separated from likely sparse authoring.

### Small fixed categories: fewer than 10

- **Item rarity values: 3 now, likely 3–5.** Metadata only.
- **Equipment slots: 5 now, likely 5–8.** Hard engine enum; body models expose a parallel capability
  vocabulary.
- **Life stages: 3 engine values.** The temporal proposal uses five balance bands, but does not approve an
  enum change.
- **Skill kinds: 2; skill categories: 3.** Stable mechanics vocabularies.
- **Place kinds: 7 proposed.** Explicit fixed planned vocabulary.
- **Module types: 7.** All are currently populated.

### Medium categories: 10–30

- **Character classes: 4 now, with 4 as the supported floor and 10 as a conservative boundary.** No plan
  names a new roster; coming of age adds selection paths.
- **Races: 13**, likely 13–20; no planned new names.
- **Terrain: 10**, **biomes: 13**, **resource types: 12**, **buildings: 9**, **services: 8**; these grow
  with world breadth, not combinatorial variants.
- **Body, cognition, drive, need, sense, combat, skill-loadout, and inventory profiles:** 6–10 now per
  family, with most projected no higher than 30.
- **Relationship roles: 3 now, with a 5–10 expected vocabulary.** Expansion is implied by marriage,
  family, and mentorship.

### Large categories: 30–100

- **Roles: 23 now**, likely 25–35; career and vacancy systems mainly reuse them.
- **Population templates: 14 now**, likely 20–35 as settlement/species coverage grows.
- **Stat profiles: 23 now**, likely 25–40 as archetype breadth grows.
- **World-event categories: 28 now**, likely 35–50 as planned features add named events.
- **World modules: 20 now**, likely 25–50; modular authoring is an intended expansion point.
- **Entity archetypes: 29 now**, practical target 40–60 to improve role/race distribution.
- **Items: 36 now**, with 36 as the supported floor and 50–80 as a conditional expansion scenario;
  additions should be counted by base ID, not stats/rarity.
- **Recipes: 25 now**, likely 35–60; alternate transformations count, numeric changes do not.
- **Quest blueprints: 40 now**, likely 50–80 as modules/scenarios broaden.
- **Authored Place identities: 0 now**, likely 20–50 initially after migration and expansion.
- **Executable skills: 4 now**, with 4 as the supported floor and 20–40 conditional on resolving profile
  labels and expanding build diversity.

### Very large categories: 100+

- **Directed race relations:** up to 156 for the fixed 13-race roster.
- **Directed faction relations:** 75 current records and a formal ceiling of 240 for 16 factions, though
  practical sparse coverage is expected.
- **Runtime named entities:** world budgets and procedural population can exceed 100 while referencing 29
  archetypes.
- **Memories, events, testimonies, and Chronicle records:** cumulative across entities and time.
- **Item instances with provenance:** only if Idea 30 is implemented broadly; the plan recommends significant
  items only, so the practical retained subset should be much smaller than all inventory instances.

### Potentially unbounded/generated categories

- Spawned entities over multiple worlds or generations.
- Runtime relationships, theoretically `N × (N - 1)` directed bonds.
- Quest opportunities and quest instances generated from world pressure.
- Camps, nests, lairs, Clans, and Households created during world history.
- Events, memories, testimonies, beliefs, local reputations, anniversaries, and Chronicle records.
- Procedurally generated world compositions, region/place layouts, item stacks, corpses, and ground objects.
- Project, objective, contract, lead, blocker, and route-option instances.

These families need per-world budgets, retention rules, or aggregation policies. They do not need inflated
authored-content targets.

## 10. Recommended Taxonomy for Future Counting

Use the following stable counting ledger. Every row should carry `authored_count`, `executable_count` where
different, `runtime_count_per_world` where measurable, `status` (current/planned/brainstorm), and `source`.

```text
Actor Content
├── SpeciesDefinition
├── BodyModelDefinition
├── RoleDefinition
├── ClassDefinition
├── EntityArchetypeDefinition
├── PopulationTemplate
└── BehaviorProfile
    ├── StatProfile
    ├── CombatProfile
    ├── CognitionProfile
    ├── DriveProfile
    ├── NeedProfile
    ├── SenseProfile
    ├── InventoryProfile
    └── SkillProfile

Economy Content
├── ItemDefinition
│   ├── Weapon
│   ├── Armor
│   ├── Trinket
│   ├── Consumable
│   ├── Tool
│   └── Material
├── ResourceNodeDefinition
├── RecipeDefinition
├── ServiceDefinition
└── BuildingDefinition

Ability and Progression Content
├── SkillDefinition
├── BreakthroughDefinition
├── ConditionDefinition (reserved; currently no registry)
└── LifecycleProfile (planned)

World Content
├── TerrainDefinition
├── BiomeDefinition
├── RegionDefinition
├── PlaceDefinition (planned)
├── EcologyDefinition
├── WorldModule
├── WorldComposition
└── SimulationScenario

Social and Institutional Content
├── FactionDefinition

├── RaceRelationRecord (planned)
├── PerspectiveProfile
├── RelationshipRoleKind
├── ContractKind

Quest and Narrative Content
├── QuestBlueprint
├── QuestBlueprintType
├── GoalKind
├── ProjectKind
├── ObjectiveKind
├── RouteFamily
├── WorldEventCategory
└── Chronicle/Memory/Belief Record Type
```

Apply these rules to future counts:

1. Count an authored definition once at its canonical ID, even if compatibility adapters expose aliases.
2. Track profiles separately from the identities that compose them.
3. Track `authored_count` and `executable_count` separately whenever data and runtime registries diverge.
4. Do not multiply base identities by rarity, level, stats, duration, stacks, equipment loadout, or seed.
5. Count a generated organization, quest, Place, person, significant item, or historical record only in a
   per-world/runtime metric, never in the authored catalog total.
6. Count directed relation records as edges, and keep their denominator explicit (`N × (N - 1)` directed
   or `N × (N - 1) / 2` unordered).
7. Preserve migration aliases and alternate schema projections as compatibility counts, not net-new content.
8. For planned content, store both a practical expected range and a hard structural ceiling when they differ.
9. Require a source path or symbol for every exact count and date each snapshot.
10. Re-run `tools/generate_content_inventory.py` for catalog totals, then separately recount embedded quest
    blueprints and hard-coded registries until those surfaces are unified.
